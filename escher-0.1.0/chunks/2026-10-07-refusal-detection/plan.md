## Goal
A driver action aimed at a target that cannot take it is refused before anything is dispatched, and the refusal
names which of the five screen-level causes holds: `not-found`, `stale`, `disabled`, `covered` or `off-screen`
(intent §6; requirements v010-11). At HEAD the executor returns `not-found` only, and a click on a disabled,
covered or off-screen target runs (research.md §Graph impact, §Patterns detected). The detection goes in front
of the click in `escher_driver`'s executor, for the two verbs that name a target by id — `click` and `type` —
and is made of readers the engine already has; a refused call leaves the held instance as it was (per
architecture §Standard Contracts → Driver session; security-plan §Input Validation, Driver command schema row).

Two decisions the entry reserved are made (`inputs#I2`). **The session remembers ids** (the operator, at the P4
forks): it keeps the id text of the screens it reads to answer a call, so an id an earlier screen read and no
element reads now is `stale`. **A sixth verb, `scroll`, joins the verb table now** (the founder's ruling,
relayed by the overseer, not provisional): it brings the element an id names into view, and the `off-screen`
remedy names it. Both stay in process: nothing of a call, an outcome or a refusal crosses the session socket,
and the crate stays silent (per obs-plan §3 Observability Harness Contract, Logging stack).

**The remedy has to be followable on the stand** (the operator's directive at the P5 review, `inputs#I3`). The
stand's scrollers are nested, so `scroll` brings its target into view through every scrolling box that holds
it, not the viewport alone; an element inside the viewport and scrolled out of such a box is refused
`off-screen`, the cause `scroll` answers; and `scroll`'s result says whether the target is in view after the
step, so a target scrolling cannot reach does not send the caller round the remedy again. The nested scroll is
built in the clean place, the engine's own `BaseDocument::scroll_into_view` — **which changes what the engine
does for every document whose scroll target sits inside a nested scrolling box: the founder's word, asked at
this plan's review** (`inputs#I3`; research.md §Patterns detected, "The clean place for the nested scroll").

The proof is on the stand: a CRUD row the driver creates past the list's box is refused, `scroll` brings it
into view, and the click then acts. Minimal in-file fixtures carry the cases the lean tasks cannot hold — a
covered element (the founder's ruling, 2026-10-06, CARRY 1), a viewport that itself scrolls, and a target no
scroll can reach (research.md §Patterns detected).

1. **The session's record of ids** — `packages/escher-driver/src/session.rs`. `Session` gains one private
   field: a bounded record of id text. It holds text ids only — no `NodeId`, no node, no bounds — starts empty at
   `Session::start`, lives as long as the session, reads no clock, and is never printed, logged, fielded or
   handed out: no public method reads it and `Hello` is untouched. Its bound is 4096 ids (a crate-private
   constant; an id is at most `MAX_ID_BYTES`, so the record is at most about 4 MiB); recording an id the record
   already holds makes it the most recently read; past the bound the id read longest ago is forgotten, and a
   forgotten id reads as one never read. `Session::start`, `with_time`, `label`, `harness`, `harness_mut` and
   `act` keep their signatures and behaviour. Inline unit tests at the end of the file, over the record alone
   with a small bound (a pure table, no held instance): an id recorded is held; one never recorded is not;
   past the bound the one read longest ago is forgotten and one re-read is kept; the row count asserted.
2. **The engine scrolls through nested boxes, tells what is in view, and the harness can ask it to** — three
   parts, the first two in `packages/blitz-dom/src/scrolling.rs` (`inputs#I3`).
   - **`BaseDocument::scroll_into_view` scrolls every scrolling box that holds its target** — its signature is
     unchanged. It walks the target's containing-block chain (`node.rs:1564-1568`) from the innermost box
     outward and, for each box that is a scrolling box by the rule a programmatic scroll already uses
     (`overflow` `scroll`, `auto` or `hidden` on the axis, `scrolling.rs:479-485`), aligns the target inside
     that box's scrollport by the requested position, with the arithmetic it already has
     (`aligned_scroll_offset`, `:588-617`), through the one-box programmatic scroll (`scroll_to`, `:555`);
     then it scrolls the viewport as it does today, from the target's position after those writes. A nested
     box is written at once whatever the behaviour asked — the engine holds one scroll animation at a time
     (`:136-140`, `:512-526`), and an outer offset is computed from where the inner one ends — and the
     requested behaviour applies to the viewport, as today. A target whose only scroller is the viewport
     scrolls exactly as before. This is the CSSOM View "scroll an element into view" order. Its three callers
     get it with no edit: script `scrollIntoView`, Dioxus `MountedData::scroll_to`, fragment navigation.
   - **One read-only reader beside it** — the part of the viewport an element can be seen through: the
     viewport's rect intersected with the scrollport of every box on the element's containing-block chain

     documented as what it is. The name is implement's (proposed: `visible_region`).

6. **The detection and the scroll** — `packages/escher-driver/src/execute.rs`, inside `Session::run`, after
   `validate` and before anything is dispatched. It replaces `centre_of` (2 callers, `run @ execute.rs:66`,
   `:70`). For the three verbs that carry an `id` — `click`, `type`, `scroll`:
   - **Read the screen** — the snapshot of the held instance, the one a settled step already takes before its
     step (`execute.rs:104`).
   - **Resolve** — the target is on the screen when that snapshot lists a node with the id AND the first pair of
     `element_ids()` with the id reads a node through `get_node` (never an index). Otherwise the call is refused
     with nothing run: `stale` when the session's record holds the id, `not-found` when it does not. An element
     that resolves but that the snapshot does not list — under `display: none`, for one — is not on the screen:
     leaned `not-found`/`stale` per `refusal.rs:60` ("no element on the screen reads the id") and `:82` ("take a
     snapshot and use an id it lists").
   - **`disabled`** (`click`, `type`) — the snapshot node's `enabled` reads `Some(false)`. Never the attribute's
     value, never focusability, never a tag or role test of the driver's own.
   - **`off-screen`** (`click`, `type`) — the centre of the node's `bounds` (viewport-relative) lies outside
     the rect step 2's reader answers for the node, or the reader answers `None`: the point is outside the
     viewport, or outside the scrollport of a box that holds the target and clips it. The executor acts at one
     point, so the reading is that point's, whatever part of the box is in view. It is read from geometry and
     never from the hit: the hit walk passes a point outside a scrolling box to that box's scrolled-out
     children (research.md §Patterns detected, "The hit walk does not clip at a scrolling box's edge").
   - **`covered`** (`click`, `type`) — the page point is that centre plus the viewport's scroll; the raw hit at
     the page point (`Harness::hit`), resolved to its element as `element_from_point` resolves it
     (`document.rs:1592-1600`: `nearest_non_anonymous_ancestor`, then up to the first element; a point over no
     box reads the root element), is neither the target nor one of its descendants by the `parent` chain.
   - **The order is fixed, one answer per call**: resolution (`stale` or `not-found`), then `disabled`, then
     `off-screen`, then `covered`. A target that is out of view is not hit-tested.
   - **The step** — `click` clicks at the page point (`Harness::click_at`); `type` clicks there and types;
     each is one settled step returning `Outcome::Acted`, as today. `scroll` calls step 2's helper on the
     resolved node and applies none of the three checks above — a disabled or covered element may be scrolled
     to. It is one settled step too and returns a variant of its own, `Outcome::Scrolled`, the acting fields
     and `in_view`: the `off-screen` reading above, made again on the screen after the step, negated — so
     `in_view` false and a following `click` refused `off-screen` are one reading, never two. A target that
     the step removed from the screen reads `in_view` false.
   - **The record** — a call `validate` refuses records nothing. Every other call records the ids of each
     snapshot it takes: the screen read for the resolution (recorded after the `stale`/`not-found` answer is
     decided), the snapshot after a settled step, the screen `snapshot` returns, and the two snapshots of a
     `press` or an `advance`.
   `press` and `advance` name no element and are not refused by this step (research.md §Patterns detected, "A

## Constraints & rejected approaches
- `BaseDocument::element_from_point` as the `covered` reader — rejected: it checks viewport coordinates and passes them unconverted to the page-coordinate hit (`document.rs:1575-1586`), so it is wrong once `scroll` has moved the viewport; the raw hit at the page point is read instead. The engine method is not edited here (one caller, blitz-vibey-script).
- Telling `covered` from overlapping snapshot bounds — rejected: stacking order, not geometry, decides what is hit (design-system §Depth Strategy).
- Detecting by dispatch — sending the click and reading the diff to decide — rejected: it breaks "refused with nothing run"; every reader the detection calls takes `&self`.
- Telling `disabled` from the attribute's value, from focusability or from a tag or role list of the driver's own — rejected (a11y-plan §5, the "keyed two ways" bullet; a11y-plan §7).
- A third dependency or a feature line in `escher-driver` to name the scroll's enums — rejected: the scroll is a harness input helper (architecture §Established Decisions → Driver session).
- A second engine into-view method beside `scroll_into_view`, leaving the old one as it is — rejected as the unclean place: the spec'd method stays wrong for its three callers and the engine carries two walks. **It is the fallback if the founder declines the change for every document**: the driver's scroll would call the new method and no existing caller's behaviour would move.
- The nested walk written in the harness helper or in the driver — rejected: a second copy of the engine's alignment arithmetic in a crate the driver ships on, and stylo's overflow values named outside the engine.
- Telling an out-of-view target from the hit, or changing the hit walk to clip at a scrolling box — rejected here: the hit passes a point to scrolled-out children (research.md §Patterns detected), and clipping it is an engine change to every pointer event that nobody asked for; the detection reads geometry before any hit. The hit walk's behaviour is recorded as a finding (Implementation notes).
- A refusal from `scroll` when it cannot bring its target into view — rejected: the directive's words are "its result says so", the step did run, and a ninth cause is barred below.
- Smooth travel of a nested box — not built: the engine holds one scroll animation at a time (`scrolling.rs:136-140`).
- A `NodeId`, a node or bounds in the session's record, or the record aged by a clock, written to the state directory or answered in `Hello` — rejected (security-plan §Input Validation, the `id` row; obs-plan §3; obs-plan §9).
- Editing a stand task, its markup or `app.rs` to manufacture a covered, off-screen or disabled case — rejected: every missing case is an in-file fixture (layout-templates §Surface: desktop-native → Primary screens).
- Refusing `press` or `advance` for a screen-level cause — not built: neither names an element, and no lean-task screen can hold a focused disabled control.
- Do not reword any cause text but the `off-screen` remedy and meaning, add a ninth cause, or let a refusal carry an id, a name, a value, a coordinate or the covering element.
