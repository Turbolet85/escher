## Goal
Two fixes in the engine, each a direct change in the engine's own files, so that what the driver reads of a scrolling
box is true before any later surface carries it out of the process (the founder's ruling, 2026-10-10 — scope.md,
CARRY 1). **A box that is scrolled reads its own bounds where it shows:** the engine's geometry reader stops counting a
box's own scroll offset against the box itself. **A hit never reaches content scrolled out of its box:** the hit walk
tests a box's own area where the box stands and stops at the edge of every box that clips by `overflow`. Both follow
the shape the founder ratified for the nested into-view scroll: the function itself, for every document, no second
function beside it (per architecture §Established Decisions → Scrolling and input).

escher-driver's logic does not change (research.md §The driver): it takes a target's centre from the snapshot's
`bounds`, `off-screen` from the visible region and `covered` from the raw hit, so both fixes reach it through the
engine. What changes in the driver is text: the three sentences that state the two limits today, with every copy of
them, return to what is then true (per architecture §Standard Contracts → Driver session; security-plan §Error
Handling). The three checks that pin the defects are restated to the true reading, and each fix gains an engine-level
check of its own. Every check is read in both layout modes and under both builds, because a workspace build and a
per-package build compile different bodies of the geometry reader (per test-plan §9 → Engine features by runner).

Decided, and by whom: the bounds fix sits in the shared reader (the operator, `inputs#I2`). The two forks written in
`.andromeda/runs/2026-10-10T13-07-20-phase/p4-founder-forks.md` were answered by the operator as technical forks — each
decides how far a fix the founder already ruled reaches, inside the principle he set (`inputs#I4`): **a hit stops at
every box that clips by `overflow`** (fork 1, option 1B), and **the second reader that carries the same shift,
`Node::absolute_position`, is measured and pinned here as it reads, its fix owned on the route** (fork 2, option 2B).
**A conformance reading is taken, report-only and bounded** (`inputs#I4`). The chunk claims no capability.

### 1. The engine-level check of the bounds reader (`tests/blitz-tests/tests/scrolled_box_client_rect.rs`, new)
In the shape of `scroll_into_view_nested`: parsed pages at 800 × 600, `for incremental in [false, true]`; a `//!`
header naming the behaviour and the bug it guards; no `cfg(unix)` gate, no `stand_` prefix, no sleep. Every box under
test carries a fixed CSS size — no assertion rests on a box sized by text. A rect is read through
`get_client_bounding_rect`; a box that is scrolled is never read through the harness's `layout_rect` or `center_of`,
which are built on the second reader (step 3). Each case first asserts that its fixture produces the condition (the
box is scrolled, by how much), then the reading. The cases, one test each:
- **A scrolled box's client rect is the rect it had before it was scrolled** — scrolled down its content, scrolled
  across it, and both.
- **A scrolled box inside a scrolled box** — the inner box's rect moves by the outer box's offset and not by its own.
- **An element inside a scrolled box** reads moved by the box's offset (as today).
- **An inline element inside a scrolling inline root** — its `node_client_rects` move with the root's scroll offset,
  the same figures before and after the fix (the caller at `document.rs:2379`).
- **An inline element whose offset parent is a scrolled, positioned box** — its `offset_rect` does not move when the
  parent is scrolled (the caller at `document.rs:2339`).
- **An element inside a scrolled box reads the same `visible_region`** before and after the box is scrolled, and that
  region is the box's padding box cut by the viewport (the caller at `scrolling.rs:776`).
The first two are the regression cases and are red on the engine as built; the other four hold before and after and
guard the three callers step 5 re-bases.

### 2. The engine-level check of the hit (`tests/blitz-tests/tests/hit_clipped_at_scrolling_box.rs`, new)
Same shape and rules as step 1; a point is computed from client rects read before the box is scrolled, never from
`layout_rect` or `center_of` of a scrolled box. The cases, one test each:
- **A point above a scrolled box, over an earlier sibling, answers the sibling** — not the box, not a row scrolled out
  of it; and the hovered node after a pointer move there is the sibling.
- **A point below an unscrolled box, where its rows overflow and nothing else stands, answers what shows there** — not
  a row.
- **A point inside the box's own area answers the row showing there**, before and after the box is scrolled.
- **An `overflow: hidden` box scrolled by a program** reads as the first case does.
- **An `overflow: clip` box** — its clipped-out content is not reached by a hit.
- **A box clipped on one axis only** (`overflow-x: clip` beside `overflow-y: visible`, the fixture first asserting
  that both values compute as written): content overflowing along the visible axis is not reached — the hit stops at
  the box's whole padding box, as paint clips it. Pinned as it reads, and named in the report beside
  `visible_region`, which narrows per axis.
- **An `overflow: visible` box** — its overflowing content is hit outside the box, as today.
- **A scrolled box's overlay scrollbar thumb is still resolved** at a point on the thumb.
- **What this fix leaves, pinned as it reads:** a `contain: paint` box whose `overflow` is `visible` — paint clips its
  content, and a hit still reaches the clipped-out content. This is the one part of the remainder this chunk
  measures; an image's, a sub-document's and a text input's own box are not measured here.
The first, second, fourth, fifth and sixth are the regression cases and are red on the engine as built.

### 6. The hit walk stops where paint clips by `overflow` (`packages/blitz-dom/src/node/node.rs`)
In `hit_inner` (`node.rs:1350-1543`), two changes and nothing else:
- **The node's own area is tested where the box stands.** Today the test is made in scrolled coordinates as
  `0 ..= size + scroll_offset` (`:1386-1389`), which admits a point up to the scroll offset above or left of a scrolled
  box. The test becomes: the point, before the scroll offset is added, lies in the node's border box.
- **A node that clips by `overflow` stops the walk at its padding box.** The predicate is the overflow half of paint's
  own, as paint has it today and nothing of the hit's own: either axis of the node's computed `overflow` is not
  `visible`, the root element excepted (`packages/blitz-paint/src/render.rs:352-358`; fork 1, option 1B —
  `inputs#I4`). Paint does not tell the two axes apart (`render.rs:324` carries upstream's own note that it should),
  so a box clipped on one axis is stopped at its whole padding box, as paint clips it. For a point outside that node's
  padding box the walk reaches none of what the node draws as children — its positive and negative z-index hoisted
  children, its paint children and its inline content — and can still answer the node itself when the point lies in
  its border box. That is paint's own order of things: paint draws all four under the clip it pushes for a clipping
  node (`render.rs:546`, `:573`, `:1018-1069` — read at P4, not measured).
The other four classes paint clips — `contain: paint`, an image, a sub-document and a text input — are **not** stops:
the hit takes paint's overflow half only (fork 1, option 1C is not taken). Inside the padding box nothing changes: the
same order (positive-z hoisted, paint children last to first, negative-z hoisted, inline content, self), the
`visibility` and `pointer-events` rules, the transform handling, the thumb resolution inside the node's own area, the
canonicalization the callers do. No `layout_data` or `layout_style()` is newly reached on a node that may lack one, no
index on an id that may be stale, no log site. A spec citation sits beside the change (CSS Overflow, the clip of a box
whose `overflow` is not `visible`). No signature changes; `hit` and `hit_with_scrollbar` keep their callers
(research.md §Graph impact: 7 production call sites).
