## Relevant amendment history
- **2026-10-19-session-recording** — the direct predecessor. It added `record-dir-not-private` and `recording-failed` to the exit-3 session-error class in §Surface: cli → Component Patterns, with no new exit and the four statuses unchanged. The plan applied it as the program's own fixed text inside a ratified carrier. Any replay failure this chunk adds to the binary has to fit the same four-status table, or it is a new exit that needs a ruling (extract constraint 3).
- **2026-10-10-driver-cli** — set the output form that this chunk's kept `answer` copies. `escher-session` prints one line of JSON per command on stdout, keyed by the schema's words, and nothing else. It uses no colour, no colour dependency, no ESC byte and no terminal test, and the exit status carries the class (0 · 1 · 2 · 3). This came from the founder's answers on the shape of stdout and on exit codes (2026-10-10). Every recording and replay run, and any replay entry point added to the binary, keeps this form. "As the driver wrote it" means this uncoloured line.
- **2026-10-10-mcp-surface** — added the `mcp` role to the same cli surface. Its stdout carries JSON-RPC lines only, uncoloured, with no ESC byte. A `snapshot` result's text is the screen text, and every other result's text is the JSON line. The shared `Speaker` asserts no ESC on every reply. This is why a kept `snapshot` answer is the driver's JSON line, not the bare MCP screen text, and why the MCP-recorded check's replay check binds to `Speaker`.
- **2026-10-12-self-description** — the four exit classes are now read from the schema's `EXIT_STATUSES` table. A session error's object carries its fixed `remedy`, and in the `mcp` role a `help` result's text is the self-description line. A replay that reads red has to stay within the `EXIT_STATUSES` statuses. A kept session-error answer, if one is kept, holds that remedy text.
- **2026-10-12-headless-screenshot** — `screenshot` answers `{"width","height","png"}` as one line. Over MCP it gives an `image` block, then a text block that is "the line without `png`". The image in band was the founder's fork 1 (2026-10-12). The same entry added the Loading rule: a shot of an app with no `sans-serif` family is refused `fonts-unavailable`. F3 B's "a shot's pixels excepted" form for a kept `screenshot` answer (no `png`, keep acceptance, width and height) matches that existing text block. A replayed shot's digest depends on the font rule.
- **2026-10-06-headless-stand** — the headless stand registers the bundled DejaVu Sans for every generic family, with system fonts off, through `HarnessOptions.font_ctx`. The point was the same layout on the dev host and on the CI runner. Each replay boot depends on this so that a recorded `screenshot` answer or digest stays stable across hosts and on two fresh boots in separate processes.
- **2026-10-07-settle-detection** — settle never waits on or advances the keeps-animating set. It returns `Settled` with `animating` read from the document's flag, and it reads animation time only through the harness's controlled clock. This is why a replay needs no sleeps or waits and gets the same settle reading in every recorded answer.
- **2026-10-07-act-by-id** — the driver's `advance` moves the app's own time through the step the session's caller hands it (on the stand, whole Timer ticks). `click`, `type` and `press` leave the clock alone. This is the origin of the `with_time` step. A replayed Timer `advance` reproduces its answer only if the replay session gets the same step the recording had. Its "leaves the animation clock alone" clause was superseded by 2026-10-18-one-virtual-clock.
- **2026-10-07-refusal-detection** — §Motion: `scroll_into_view` writes every nested box at once, and the driver's `scroll` is instant inside one settled step. This is why a recorded `scroll` step's answer (its diff and in-view reading) replays deterministically and has no smooth-scroll timing to reproduce.
- **2026-10-18-one-virtual-clock** — the harness drives animation from the `VirtualClock` its documents read. The overlay scrollbar fade, smooth scrolling and fling read that clock. `advance` moves it by exactly its `ms` and moves the app's time through the session's step, and after a driver `scroll`, `animating` reads true until 700 ms of that clock pass. A replay has to boot on the harness's `VirtualClock`, not the wall clock (the extract's anti-pattern). Because `animating` and settle readings depend on that clock, a recorded answer reproduces only when the replay runs the same sequence of `advance` steps.
- **2026-10-18-engine-debts** (the second entry, the PROVISIONAL batch ratified at the Epoch 2 close) — the founder ratified the one-virtual-clock §Motion clause (2026-10-18), along with the hit stop at a `contain: paint` box and the cursor fix. The clock behaviour a replay relies on is therefore settled, not PROVISIONAL. The other two clauses do not bear on this chunk.
