The drop must therefore hold at every level `RUST_LOG` can name; whether a `RUST_LOG` directive naming a third-party target could re-admit such a record under the chosen mechanism is research's question, and the plan's unqualified wording says it must not.
- security-plan §Logging & Monitoring (escher's own sink) requires the existing scrub to stand unweakened beside the drop: engine-target records print only their safe fields, content-named fields are redacted at every target that still prints, bridged `log` records pass the same rules, and the sink writes to stderr only. - security-plan §Input Validation (Markup attributes · `aria-label` · `<label for>`, accessible names) requires that a sink-installing host write no accessible name to stderr; the row records one printing at `trace` through a third-party target. ## Patterns to follow
- Judge a bridged `log` record by the target it was logged under, the way security-plan §Logging & Monitoring (escher's own sink) states the scrub already treats bridged records — the drop and the scrub apply one rule to native and bridged records alike.
- Keep the allowlist closed and prefix-named, each admitted target carrying a scrub rule of its own, as security-plan §Logging & Monitoring (escher's own sink) describes the engine list: a target is admitted only together with the rule that says what of it may print.
- Spawn the host through its closed argv and a private state directory, per security-plan §Input Validation (CLI arguments · escher-session; Driver session state directory): the state directory is a parameter under the build-time target temp directory, never an env read or a cwd-relative default, and the host's own stderr writes are its fixed usage and error messages, which carry no path, label, id or value — the check can tell those from a leaked record.
- Record the by-level measurement as form and counts with its evidence path cited, as security-plan §Logging & Monitoring (escher's own sink) cites the prior reading, and keep raw captured stderr out of committed files, as that section's agent-run and cold-agent bullets keep raw unscrubbed logs under `target/`.
- If the drop needs a manifest change, follow security-plan §Dependency Security (Pinning; Lockfile verification under Supply chain integrity): versions inherited from the workspace, `Cargo.lock` committed, builds `--locked`, and the audit leg re-run.

## Anti-patterns to avoid
- Admitting a third-party target (the Stylo, selectors or dioxus-core families the plan names) to the allowlist without a scrub rule, or printing its records "as written" behind a level — that reopens exactly the leak security-plan §Logging & Monitoring (escher's own sink) records. (security-plan §Security Anti-Patterns reads NO RECORDED INTENT; nothing is cited from it.)
- Replacing the formatter's scrub with the drop, or relaxing the content-named redaction for escher's own targets because they are "ours" — security-plan §Logging & Monitoring (escher's own sink) states content-named fields are redacted at any target.
- Widening the claim past the measurement: stating the open clause closed for typed text, for the upstream apps or the WPT runner, for the chained panic hook's raw message or for the host path in `log.file`, none of which security-plan §Bootstrap phases (`logging-redaction-wire`) or §Logging & Monitoring assign to this entry. Whether the `log.file` host path narrows as a side effect is research's question and is recorded only as measured.
