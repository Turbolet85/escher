# layouts extract

## Relevance
partial — the chunk adds state attributes (and, by decision 2, possibly roles) to controls of two lean stand tasks and widens what the `escher-session` answer holds; it creates no surface, region or component. Each surface header's `> NOT YET MEASURED` line names gaps (tooling context, expression level, signature placement, hero) and nothing is extracted for those; §Decisions Log reads `> NO RECORDED INTENT` and is not cited.

## Constraints
- The stand's markup additions are attribute-only: layout-templates requires that a lean task's author `id`s and its naming attributes add no element, class, style, wrapper or order. The chunk's three state attributes on the flight mode buttons, the two date inputs and the CRUD rows are held to that same shape (per layout-templates §Surface: desktop-native → Primary screens, the seven_guis Home and TaskShell bullet). Whether a role chosen at decision 2 can be stated without a new element or a moved one is research's question; a role that needs a wrapper or a re-parented list is a departure from this section and is flagged to the wrap, never made silently.
- The headless stand's mount is fixed: one lean task in TaskShell through `task_in_shell`, the shell's header-over-body structure and its ids, at the pinned viewport, scale, colour scheme and bundled font the section names. The chunk's checks and its measured-first snapshots run on that mount, and it holds after the change in both layout modes (per layout-templates §Surface: desktop-native → Primary screens, the seven_guis Home and TaskShell bullet).
- Every control the chunk touches keeps its author `id` as its stable element id — the flight booker's mode buttons and date inputs, the CRUD list and each CRUD row, whose id stands beside its Dioxus key and follows its person under filter, Create and Delete. A state attribute or a role changes none of them (per layout-templates §Surface: desktop-native → Primary screens, the seven_guis Home and TaskShell bullet).
- The `escher-session` host is a consumer of the same mount that adds no markup, id, class, style, wrapper or order, and a held instance reads as a fresh boot. The three states therefore reach a hosted snapshot from the task's own markup, never from anything the host adds (per layout-templates §Surface: desktop-native → Primary screens, the seven_guis Home and TaskShell bullet).
- The CRUD list is the stand's nested scrolling box and the box that clips its rows; the section records its boot bounds, that those bounds hold after its content is scrolled, and how a driver `click` and `scroll` read against it. A container role on the list or an item role on its rows must leave those readings as the section states them (per layout-templates §Surface: desktop-native → IA notes, the scrolling-boxes bullet). Whether the code's readings still match after the change is research's and verify's question.
- The driver's command line keeps its shape: one command per run, `--session` once, flags spelt as the schema spells them, four endings, and an accepted answer as one line of uncoloured JSON on stdout. The chunk's new state keys live inside that one line; it adds no verb, flag, ending, stream or line (per layout-templates §Surface: cli → Primary screens, the `escher-session` bullet).
- Cells is not on the headless surface: the headless stand skips Home and mounts one of four lean tasks, and Cells is reached only from Home's seventh card, which the section places below Home's scrolling box at the stand's viewport, the windowed stand not measured. Decision 6 is taken knowing that no headless check of the chunk can read a Cells state (per layout-templates §Surface: desktop-native → Primary screens, the seven_guis Home and TaskShell bullet).

## Patterns to follow
- Naming by attributes alone on existing elements — the section's precedent is `for` on existing labels and `aria-label` on the two date inputs, with no element, class, style or order added. The two date inputs that take `aria-invalid` are the same two elements (per layout-templates §Surface: desktop-native → Primary screens, the seven_guis Home and TaskShell bullet).
- The author `id` is the stable element id, and no task CSS selects by it: a check names its target by the listed ids, not by class or position (per layout-templates §Surface: desktop-native → Primary screens, the seven_guis Home and TaskShell bullet).
- A CRUD row is addressed as `crud-person-{person.id}`, the fixture's three rows and the first created row as the section lists them — the ids a "selected row and no other row" check reads (per layout-templates §Surface: desktop-native → Primary screens, the seven_guis Home and TaskShell bullet).
- A document mounts into the fixed skeleton's `main`, and the harness's default viewport is the stand's pinned one — a minimal fixture beside the stand, if the chunk needs one, takes the same skeleton and viewport (per layout-templates §Surface: desktop-native → IA notes, the skeleton and test-harness bullets).

## Anti-patterns to avoid
- Adding, wrapping, re-parenting or reordering an element in a lean task to carry a state or a role, or moving the visual state from its class onto the new attribute — the section holds every stand addition to no element, class, style, wrapper or order (per layout-templates §Surface: desktop-native → Primary screens, the seven_guis Home and TaskShell bullet).
- Having the host role, the boot or the shell add markup or attributes so that a hosted screen reads differently from a fresh boot (per layout-templates §Surface: desktop-native → Primary screens, the seven_guis Home and TaskShell bullet).
- Writing the new states anywhere but inside the answer's one JSON line — a second stdout line, a new ending or an exit status of its own (per layout-templates §Surface: cli → Primary screens, the `escher-session` bullet).
